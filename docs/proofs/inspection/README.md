# Inspection review proof (synthetic thin report)

Synthetic data only. This is what a homeowner's review looks like for a thin report: material questions first, then the rest.

## Questions to ask the inspector

### Page 1 (the five the report leans on most)

- In Living Room, the report recommends work but doesn't show a reading or sample for that area. Ask: What supports the recommendation there?
  - Why: Work that is opened up or removed should rest on something measured or sampled.
- In Living Room, the report states a cause but doesn't show a reading for that area. Ask: What measurement points to that cause?
  - Why: A cause is easier to rely on with a reading behind it.
- Some conclusions don't point to the finding behind them. Ask: What supports this conclusion?
  - Share request: Could you share the supporting photo, reading or sample?
  - Why: Each conclusion points to a photo, reading or sample ID.
- A cause is stated firmly. Ask: How confident is it, and what supports it?
  - Share request: Could you share your supporting notes?
  - Why: Cause of moisture is stated as confirmed, probable, possible or not determined.
- Something plausible is described as the cause. Ask: What shows it is the cause and not just a possible one?
  - Why: A plausible contributor is kept separate from a demonstrated cause.

### Notes (everything else, material points first)

- Remediation is recommended before the moisture source is addressed. Ask: What fixes the source first, and who does it?
  - Why: Moisture source correction comes before remediation, with the right trade named.
- The report calls the wall wet, but doesn't show what it was compared to. Ask: What dry reading did you compare this to?
  - Share request: Could you share the dry reference reading?
  - Why: A dry standard was taken on site from a like material, or a general standard is named.
- The report doesn't explain why each sample was taken where it was. Ask: What was each sample meant to test?
  - Share request: Could you share your sampling plan?
  - Why: Each sample has a stated purpose tied to the question.
- I couldn't confirm that sample IDs and times match between the field log, custody form and lab report. Ask: Can I see them side by side?
  - Share request: Could you share the field log, chain of custody and lab report?
  - Why: Sample IDs, locations, times and volumes match among field log, custody form and lab report.
- The numbers in the report can be compared to the lab sheet. Ask: Can I see the original lab report?
  - Share request: Could you share the original lab PDF?
  - Why: The original lab report is attached and its numbers match the report's tables.
- The summary and the lab table don't agree on where the highest result came from. Worth asking which is right.
  - Share request: Could you share the lab report and chain of custody?
  - Why: Each lab value sits under the correct location and sample ID.
- Results in different units are compared directly. Ask: How were these made comparable?
  - Why: Results in different units or methods (spores/m3, CFU/m3, DNA, surface) aren't compared as if equal.
- The conclusions seem to lean on sample results alone. Ask: How do the visual and moisture findings fit in?
  - Share request: Could you share your reasoning?
  - Why: Interpretation combines what was seen, moisture, history and samples; samples are not read alone.
- The report draws conclusions about an area it doesn't say was entered. Ask: Was this area inspected?
  - Share request: Could you share your notes on access?
  - Why: Conclusions are limited to areas the assessor entered.
- Some conclusions read stronger than the visit's limits would support. Ask: What remains unresolved, and what would resolve it?
  - Why: Conclusions are no stronger than the limits allow, and the report says what is still unresolved.
- The conclusions don't clearly answer the original question. Ask: What is the answer to the question this assessment set out to address?
  - Why: Conclusions answer the stated objectives.
- These categories are blended together. Ask: Which areas are confirmed, which suspected, and which clear?
  - Why: Confirmed growth, suspected hidden growth, moisture without growth and no findings are kept distinct.
- The limitations are listed but don't say how they affect the conclusions. Ask: Which conclusions are limited by them?
  - Why: Job-specific limitations explain their effect on each affected conclusion.
- The scope of work may be larger than the area shown. Ask: How does the scope relate to the measured growth?
  - Why: Remediation scope matches the measured area of growth.
- The report doesn't say how success will be judged after remediation. Ask: What will be checked, and what counts as clear?
  - Why: Post-remediation checks are defined in advance (visual, moisture, sampling).
- The report doesn't say how dust will be contained. Ask: Is that left to the remediator's plan?
  - Why: Isolation, dust control, HVAC protection and debris routes are addressed, or left to the remediator's own plan.
- The report describes wet or damp conditions. Ask: Where exactly, and how was that determined?
  - Share request: Could you share your readings?
  - Why: Anything called wet or damp is identified.
- The readings aren't laid out by location. Ask: Can I see the readings by location?
  - Share request: Could you share your moisture map or table?
  - Why: Readings are reported with location, material, value and scale (table or map).
- It isn't clear whether the moisture is current or from the past. Ask: Is the moisture active today?
  - Share request: Could you share your readings and notes?
  - Why: The report says whether moisture is active or historical.
- The readings don't say which scale they use, which changes what they mean. Ask: Which scale are these on?
  - Share request: Could you share the meter settings?
  - Why: The report says whether readings are actual moisture content, a wood-equivalent or a relative scale.
- The report picks a cause for a stain without comparing other explanations. Ask: What else could explain it?
  - Why: Other plausible explanations were considered (roof, plumbing, condensation).
- Something is called wet or damp, but no reading is shown. Ask: What was measured, and where?
  - Share request: Could you share the meter details?
  - Why: A wet or damp call is easier to rely on with a reading beside it.
- The method may not fit the question being asked. Ask: Why was this method chosen?
  - Share request: Could you share your plan?
  - Why: The sampling method fits the question (tape for visible growth, cavity or spore trap for air).
- The sample locations aren't shown on a plan or photo. Ask: Where exactly was each taken?
  - Share request: Could you share your floor plan and photos?
  - Why: Sample locations are shown on a plan or photos.
- I couldn't find the chain of custody. Ask: Can I see it?
  - Share request: Could you share the chain-of-custody form?
  - Why: A complete, signed chain of custody matches the report's sample IDs.
- The report doesn't show units or detection limits clearly. Ask: What are the units and the lowest amount the lab can detect?
  - Share request: Could you share the lab report?
  - Why: Units, raw counts and detection limits are understood and reported correctly.
- Lab comments don't seem to carry into the interpretation. Ask: Were there lab notes about these samples?
  - Share request: Could you share the lab report?
  - Why: Lab comments and qualifiers appear in the interpretation.
- The report uses 'none' or 'absent' where it may mean 'not seen'. Ask: What area and method does this cover?
  - Why: 'Not detected', 'not observed', 'not assessed' and 'absent' are used carefully, with area and time.
- Other possible explanations aren't discussed. Ask: What else could explain these results?
  - Why: Outdoor entry, recent disturbance, cleaning and debris are considered.
- The report doesn't describe how the building was walked through. Ask: Which spaces were inspected, and in what order?
  - Share request: Could you share your inspection notes?
  - Why: The inspection approach is described (room by room, attic, mechanical, crawlspace, exterior).
- Some areas don't say what was seen there. Ask: What was observed in each area, including where nothing was found?
  - Share request: Could you share your field notes and photos?
  - Why: Each area records growth (where, size, material), staining, damage and conditions.
- The report doesn't describe staining or discoloration where it matters. Ask: Were any stains or dark areas seen, and where?
  - Share request: Could you share your photos?
  - Why: Staining, discoloration and dark areas are described with location.
- The report doesn't say whether materials showed swelling, rust, rot or warping. Ask: What condition were the materials in?
  - Share request: Could you share your photos?
  - Why: Swelling, rust, rot, warping or crumbling is described.
- The report doesn't say whether hidden areas were opened. Ask: Which hidden areas were opened, and which weren't?
  - Share request: Could you share your photos or notes?
  - Why: Whether hidden areas (behind cabinets, in walls) were opened or only noted.
- The report doesn't say which areas were checked and found clear. Ask: Which areas were examined with nothing found, and which weren't examined?
  - Why: Areas where nothing was found are recorded, and unexamined spaces aren't described as unaffected.
- The report doesn't describe the building's water history. Ask: What water problems were reported, and when?
  - Share request: Could you share the interview notes?
  - Why: Leaks, floods, storms, repairs and earlier remediation are noted.
- It isn't always clear what the inspector saw and what someone told them. Ask: Which statements are firsthand observations?
  - Why: What the assessor saw, what occupants or contractors said, records reviewed and the assessor's own inferences are kept apart.
- The report doesn't say whether anything was cleaned or treated before the visit. Ask: Was any cleaning, drying or treatment done before the inspection?
  - Share request: Could you share your interview notes?
  - Why: Recent cleaning, drying, demolition or antimicrobial use is noted.
- The report doesn't state why the assessment was done. Ask: What question was this assessment meant to answer?
  - Share request: Could you share the signed scope or agreement?
  - Why: The reason is stated (complaint, visible growth, water event, sale, other).
- It isn't clear which areas were and weren't covered. Ask: Which areas were included and which were left out?
  - Share request: Could you share the scope of work?
  - Why: Areas included and excluded are defined.
- The report doesn't show the question the sampling was meant to test. Ask: What was each sample meant to answer?
  - Share request: Could you share your notes on the plan?
  - Why: A working question or hypothesis is stated.
- I couldn't compare what was done to what was agreed. Ask: Did the work match the agreed scope, and what changed?
  - Share request: Could you share the signed scope of work?
  - Why: What was delivered matches the agreement, and changes are explained.
- The report's title and conclusions sound broader than the service it describes. Ask: Was this a full assessment, a limited visit, or sampling only?
  - Share request: Could you share the signed scope of work?
  - Why: The kind of service (full assessment, limited visit, sampling only) matches the report's title and conclusions.
- The report doesn't say who set the limits on time, rooms or samples. Ask: Which limits came from the client and which from the inspector?
  - Share request: Could you share the scope or any change notes?
  - Why: Limits on time, rooms, access, sample count and methods, and who set each.
- The limitations are general and don't name the areas that weren't reached. Ask: Which areas weren't accessible?
  - Share request: Could you share your access notes?
  - Why: Limitations name the real inaccessible areas, not only boilerplate.
- There's no plan showing where things were found. Ask: Can I see a sketch?
  - Share request: Could you share your sketch?
  - Why: A plan shows sample locations, readings and growth areas.
- The report's dates and identifying details are worth a quick match-up, since they don't all line up. Ask: Which dates and address are correct?
  - Share request: Could you send the corrected page or your inspection log?
  - Why: Address, client, report date, inspection date and version agree throughout.
- The report doesn't say whether the HVAC was running or the building was closed up during the visit. Ask: What was the state of the building and HVAC?
  - Share request: Could you share your field notes?
  - Why: Occupied or vacant, HVAC running or not, and windows and doors are recorded.
- The report gives two different dates. Ask: Why was sampling on a different day than the inspection?
  - Why: Conditions can change between days, and results describe the day sampled.
  - Report, page 2: "Date of Inspection: July 25, 2025"
  - Report, page 2: "Sampled: 07-27-25"
- The report names more than one laboratory. Ask: Which lab analyzed which sample?
  - Why: Each result should trace to the lab that produced it.
  - Report, page 2: "Samples are sent to a 3rd party laboratory (AEML Inc.) for analysis."
  - Report, page 2: "Laboratory: Sporelytics"
- The report doesn't mention anything that could skew the readings. Ask: Were any such effects considered?
  - Why: Metal, salts, coatings, thickness and surface effects were considered.
- The report doesn't mention field blanks. Ask: Were any collected?
  - Share request: Could you share the blank results?
  - Why: Field blanks were used where the method calls for them.
- The report doesn't mention that a single sample reflects one moment. Ask: How representative are these results?
  - Why: One-time sampling and spore variability are acknowledged.
- The report doesn't say whether there was an odor. Ask: Was there a musty or other smell, and where?
  - Share request: Could you share your field notes?
  - Why: Whether there was an odor, where, and what kind.
- The report gives little about how the building is built. Ask: Can you describe the construction and HVAC system?
  - Share request: Could you share your notes?
  - Why: Age, construction, roof, cladding and HVAC type are described.
- The report doesn't show what the occupants told the assessor. Ask: What did the occupants report and when did it start?
  - Share request: Could you share your interview notes?
  - Why: When symptoms or odors started and where, recorded without medical claims.
- Some photos aren't labeled with what they show. Ask: Can I have the labels?
  - Share request: Could you share the photo log?
  - Why: Photos are labeled with location, finding and date, and referenced in the text.
- The report doesn't show the weather on the day, which matters for outdoor comparisons. Ask: What were the outdoor conditions?
  - Share request: Could you share your field notes?
  - Why: Weather and outdoor conditions on the day are recorded.
