# Lou's plain-words terms (drafts; each needs Cory's approval)

## 1. Moisture reading (APPROVED by Cory 2026-11-16)
What it is: A number from a meter that shows how much moisture is held inside a material, like drywall, wood or concrete.
Where it fits: Mold needs moisture, so readings usually come early, before anyone looks for mold. A reading by itself means little. It means something when compared with a dry reference, a reading of the same kind of material somewhere unaffected. Readings tell about moisture inside materials. They do not show whether water is still arriving.
You could ask: "Which rooms did you take readings in, what were the numbers, and what dry reference did you compare them to?"
How it connects: Moisture readings point to where water may be, an air sample shows what is floating in the air, and remediation is the cleanup if there is a problem.

## 0. Types of water (APPROVED by Cory 2026-11-16)
What it is: Water shows up in three ways in a building. Water vapor is water in the air, which humidity measures. Absorbed moisture is water held inside a material like wood or drywall, even when it looks dry. Bulk water is liquid you can see or feel, like a leak or a puddle.
Where it fits: Inspectors look for the source of the water first, because mold needs moisture to grow. A report should say which kind was found and where it came from.
You could ask: "Which kind of water did you find, and where do you think it came from?"
How it connects: Bulk water can soak into materials and become absorbed moisture, and wet materials can release vapor into the air. Moisture readings mostly measure the middle one.

## 2. Spore count (awaiting approval)
What it is: A lab number showing how many mold spores were caught in a sample of air, often given as spores per cubic meter of air.
Where it fits: Labs get air samples from inside the home and from outdoors. Inspectors compare indoor to outdoor and room to room, because spores are always in the air. A count by itself does not say whether a home is safe.
You could ask: "What did you compare the indoor counts to, and where was the outdoor sample taken?"
How it connects: An air sample is how you get a spore count, and a moisture reading helps explain why one room might have more.
